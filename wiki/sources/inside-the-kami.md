## Summary
A narrower companion to the 6-Pack manifesto: this essay surveys what ML substrate makes Civic AI governance easier to uphold. It argues that Yoshua Bengio's *Scientist AI* (truth-without-appetite, "epistemic correctness" via a truthification pipeline) and Yann LeCun's *Superhuman Adaptable Intelligence* (the agenda for goal-bounded but adaptable agents) both point toward bounded, specialised systems rather than a single general-purpose governor.

## Key claims

- Technology-agnosticism is not technology-indifference: a deceptive model turns oversight into permanent combat; a general-purpose optimiser strains every boundary; an opaque system makes the *Competence* pack (Pack 3) impossible to verify.
- Bengio's truthification pipeline trains models to distinguish "X is true" from "someone wrote X" — separating the world from rhetoric about it.
- LeCun's path: goal-bounded but locally adaptable agents are easier to govern than universal agents.
- The technical convergence does not settle politics. "The inside still cannot decide legitimacy, standing, pace, or justice. Those remain institutional questions."
