## Summary
Tang frames the Civic AI argument as a personal scene: aboard a flight in airplane mode, she runs **jdd-kami** — a bounded Civic AI cultivated with co-creator Tenzin Yangtso — on local hardware, asks it to "write about democracy," and it writes about *listening*. The essay contrasts this with the "default trajectory" of one general-intelligence singleton that promises to render politics unnecessary.

## Key claims

- A Kami "cultivated by how we care about each other and the world" returns to "the thing care always centres on: attention to a relationship."
- The default trajectory: "One powerful system. One general intelligence. Trained on everything, governing everything, optimising everything — from above. A singleton so capable it renders politics unnecessary. In this story, democracy is not destroyed. It is simply … outgrown."
- A bounded local Kami runs on hardware "we hold, inspect and can shut down" — pi.audreyt.org references a guide for running a frontier model with stable, contestable decision traces.
- Connects to the Civic AI Conference 2026 at Rhodes House, Oxford (25 March 2026), where Glen Weyl was among the keynote speakers.
