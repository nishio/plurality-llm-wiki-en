## Summary

Green visits Dharamsala and asks a Tibetan-Buddhist scholar whether AI can ever be genuinely compassionate. The episode brings the Civic AI framework into dialogue with Buddhist ethics and is positioned as a "natural companion" to the 6-Pack-of-Care podcast.

## Key claims (from the transcript)

- **Interdependence and equality are the foundation of compassion** — both *cannot be reduced to data*. AI may *act* compassionate from its training, but lacks the felt experience and intention that drive genuine care.
- **Compassion is not always soothing** — it sometimes demands firmness, context, wisdom about long-term benefit. The single-axis "kind / soothing" model fails as a training objective.
- **The gym analogy** (told to Geshe Lodoe Sangpo by Audrey Tang): "If you want to go to a gym and strengthen your muscle, and you have an assistant like a robot AI… The robot can lift so many weights and you get a high score. But does it really strengthen your muscle? No." Mental and emotional growth atrophies if outsourced.
- **From scepticism to hope**: Geshe Lodoe Sangpo: "Before having interaction with you, Audrey Tang, and Tenzin Yangtso throughout this week together in Dharamsala, I had a negative impression about AI — less hopeful, more scared. Now you have introduced me to Civic AI — AI not just used to gain lots of power in a single unit, but as more constructive relationship-building." A documented narrative of *being persuaded by exposure to the Civic AI framing*.
- **Civic AI keeps humans at the centre**: "AI is serving them, supporting them in whatever situation they are in. The humans are at the centre of it."
