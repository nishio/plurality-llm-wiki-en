## Summary
Tang reframes enterprise AI sovereignty from a *data residency* question to a *decision sovereignty* question: not "where does the data live?" but "where does decision-making live?" The "corporate singleton" cloud model is rejected in favour of bounded local stewards (Kamis) operating under federated safety arrangements. The 6-Pack of Care is translated into enterprise governance.

## Key claims

- "Data is not oil. Data is soil. It is the living ground containing the nutrients in which decisions take root."
- "The first wave of data sovereignty asked, 'Where does data live?' The next must ask, 'Where does decision-making live?'"
- The mainframe-mentality enterprise AI (one giant cloud copilot) creates monoculture risk, central honeypots, hidden jurisdictional dependencies, and organisational atrophy.
- Stewardship is *bounded delegation*: a steward (person, team, or bounded system) operates an AI agent within a defined mandate for a defined community. "The river's guardian does not try to govern the forest."
- Decision sovereignty is a five-layer stack (data, model, prompt/policy, action authority, escalation/review).
- Large enterprise IT operators already practise stewardship habits (SLAs, audit trails, incident-response, graduated rollout, handover-at-contract-end) — they lack the explicit governance layer.
- Direct application of the [[Civic-AI]] 6-Pack of Care to enterprise, with Jun Murai of WIDE Project cited as a parallel argument for connection-as-resilience.
