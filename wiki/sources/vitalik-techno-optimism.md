## Bibliographic Info

- **Title**: *My techno-optimism*
- **Author**: Vitalik Buterin

- **Form**: blog post (long-form essay)

## Summary

Written as a response to [[Marc-Andreessen]]'s "techno-optimist manifesto," the essay proposes a *differential* techno-optimism: not all technology directions are equally beneficial, and human intention is needed to steer toward defensive and democratising directions rather than the "maximise profit" gradient. Buterin coins "d/acc" — variously *defensive*, *decentralised*, *democratic*, *differential* acceleration — and explicitly aligns it with the [[Plurality]] book project as cousins-on-different-fronts.

## Key claims

- The Andreessen manifesto unambiguously rejects "ideology of stagnation"; Buterin agrees on magnitude (against stagnation) but disagrees on direction (some tech is more world-improving than others).
- **Four d/acc fronts** mapping to Andreessen's "four horsemen": *world defense* (drones, defensive bio), *bio defense* (open vaccines, indoor air quality, wastewater surveillance), *cyber defense / info defense* (formal verification, open OS, bridging tools), and *political defense* (Plurality-style governance).
- **Info defense** explicitly references Audrey Tang and the Plurality book: "Plurality-style technology, in which 'bridging' algorithms reward people for understanding each other rather than for going viral, is a key ingredient."
- AI risk is *real* (not dismissed) but the answer is not pause-or-ban — it is *making humans smarter and more coordinated* via better social technology so we can manage AI together.
- Closing: "I am a fan of techno-optimism, but I am a *technology-direction-specific* techno-optimist. I want to see the world get better through technologies that *empower the meek*."

## Significance for the wiki

This is the essay where Buterin most directly positions himself as a *Plurality fellow-traveler from the web3 side*. It supplies the d/acc vocabulary that has since become a shared reference point in the Tang/Weyl/Buterin axis. Importantly, it also marks a divergence in vocabulary: d/acc is *defensive* and *differential*, while Plurality is *positive-sum / collaborative*. The two frames are compatible but emphasise different things — d/acc is more concerned with catastrophic-risk reduction, Plurality more with cultivating diversity-amid-connection.
