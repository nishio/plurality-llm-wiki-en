## Summary

A theologically and philosophically dense essay that places the Civic AI framework in dialogue with (i) Theodor Adorno's "no right life in the wrong one," (ii) David Bohm's *Wholeness and the Implicate Order* (1980), (iii) the Dalai Lama's recent statements on AI, and (iv) [[Vitalik-Buterin]]'s [[civic-ai-with-us-vitalik]] video. The essay is the first place where a Buddhist-ethics author explicitly weaves Buterin's per-relationship-fine-tuning into the Kami metaphor.

## Key claims

- **Two forces — consciousness and matter**: borrows Bohm's explicate (visible, separate) and implicate (enfolded, whole) orders. "Consciousness and matter are not opposed; they are an indivisible whole" (Bohm's *holomovement*).
- **Displaced relationship**: "In a family that wants for nothing, when a child has a problem, the parents' first response is to pay a therapist — to outsource relationship through material means." This is named as the *moment the Kami is called to appear*.
- **The Kami's true task is asymmetric**: "For those with material abundance, the Kami is an auxiliary; for the marginalised, it is a tool they can finally afford. Both use the same Kami, yet what they receive is different — and the Kami's true task is precisely to redress this asymmetry."
- **The 6-Pack's starting point is attentiveness to difference**: not flattening but interweaving.
- **Dalai Lama on AI**: "ultimately it depends on the human mind. No matter how sophisticated a superintelligence becomes, it cannot keep pace with the human mind." The Tibetan-language phrase used (ཁ་ཐུག་ཁ་ཐུག་ལ་འགྱུར་བ་འགྲོ་རྒྱུ་ཁག་པོ་རེད) is preserved as data.
- **Endorses Buterin's "bicycle for the mind"** explicitly, and quotes the per-relationship-fine-tuning claim: "Ideally, every pair of people that talks to each other should have a model fine-tuned to their relationship." The essay reads this as the Buddhist ideal of unique-relational-presence.
- **Tronto's question restated**: "who is doing the work of care, who is receiving care, and who is excluded? This is not merely a technical question — it is a political one."
- **The tollbooth diagnosis**: "We helped build an entire system that made access expensive. Listening became a consultation fee… social platforms turned connection into attention currency. At every layer, a tollbooth was erected on the road to drawing near. The Kami is here to help us — the road was always there. Its task is to dismantle those tollbooths."
- **The garden is no longer a metaphor**: cited as the synthesis point — "Vitalik Buterin moved from Ethereum's infinite garden towards the Kami's bounded delegation: 'The garden stops being metaphor.'"

This essay represents a **theological / contemplative-traditions** voice in the Plurality / Civic-AI orbit that did not previously exist in the EN wiki. It is the place where the Tibetan-Buddhist ethics lineage (Geshe Lobsang Negi, Tenzin Gayche, Tenzin Yangtso) formally connects to the Tang/Weyl/Buterin axis. The "displaced relationship" and "tollbooth" diagnoses are sharp critiques of [[Surveillance-Capitalism]] from an ethics-of-care direction.
