## Bibliographic Info

- **Title**: *Plurality: The Future of Collaborative Technology and Democracy*
- **Authors**: E. Glen Weyl, Audrey Tang, and the Plurality Community
- **Edition consulted**: English edition (full book PDF, 2024 print release at the time of ingest)
- **License**: Creative Commons CC0 — no rights reserved; freely reusable
- **Online home**: https://www.plurality.net/
- **Source repository**: https://github.com/pluralitybook/plurality
- **Note on the book's logo**: a Unicode glyph standing in for the concept Plurality (also written as 數位, *shuwei*, meaning both "digital" and "plural" in Mandarin) appears throughout the original layout; this wiki spells the concept out as "Plurality" or "Plural" depending on context.

## Authors

- [[Glen-Weyl]] — founder of RadicalxChange, of Microsoft Research's Plural Technology Collaboratory and the Plurality Institute, and co-author of *Radical Markets*.
- [[Audrey-Tang]] — inaugural Minister of Digital Affairs of Taiwan and inaugural "digital" minister of the Plurality Institute community.
- The book is explicitly community-authored under a CC0 community-governance protocol the authors call ⿻ Management Protocol, with dozens of named contributors tracked via community credit tokens.

## Structural Overview

The book is organized in seven sections. It is written in a deliberately "circular" rather than linear style: the preface recommends readers with different interests (current affairs, intellectual, technical, applications, impact, policy) start in different parts of the book.

### Section 1: Preface
- **1 Seeing Plural** — Frames the project: technology and democracy at a crossroads; in Mandarin 數位 means both "digital" and "plural"; democracy as a "river of hope" needing replenishment.

### Section 2: Introduction
- **2-0 Information Technology and Democracy: a Widening Gulf** — Two opposing threats from tech: "anti-social" (eroding social fabric) and "centralizing" (mass surveillance, foundation models). Diagnoses social, economic, political, legal, and existential faces of the anti-social threat.
- **2-1 A View From Yushan** — The Taiwan story. Sunflower movement, [[Sun-Yat-Sen]]'s [[Tridemism]], [[John-Dewey]]'s pragmatism, post-WWII rural land reform, civil-society cooperative tradition, and the rise of [[Digital-Democracy]] in Taiwan.
- **2-2 The Life of a Digital Democracy** — Concrete Taiwanese digital-democracy practices: [[vTaiwan]], [[Join]], Pol.is bridging, mask map / COVID response.

### Section 3: Plurality
- **3-0 What is Plurality?** — The three-part definition: descriptive (Hannah Arendt), normative (Danielle Allen), prescriptive (Audrey Tang's 數位/digital-plural). Plurality = "technology for collaboration across social difference"; contrasts with "monist atomism" of Libertarianism and Technocracy.
- **3-1 Living in a Plural World** — Twentieth-century science's shift from [[Monist-Atomism|monist atomism]] to complexity, networks, ecosystems, relationality (physics, biology, neuroscience, network sociology a la [[Mark-Granovetter]]).
- **3-2 Connected Society** — Danielle Allen's "Connected Society"; intersecting non-hierarchical affiliations; identity as bundles of group memberships.
- **3-3 [[The-Lost-Dao]]** — Pre-history of the internet's Plural ambitions: [[JCR-Licklider]]'s "Intergalactic Computer Network", [[Ted-Nelson]], [[Doug-Engelbart]], ARPANET; how the vision was lost to private corporate platforms.

### Section 4: Freedom
Articulates Plurality as both a system of rights and a digital operating system, structured as a "hypergraph" of people, groups, and digital assets.
- **4-0 Rights, Operating Systems and ⿻ Freedom** — Frames OSs as analogous to systems of rights; both are dynamic, networked, aspirational foundations.
- **4-1 [[Identity-and-Personhood]]** — Networked, intersectional identity; verifiable credentials; decentralized identifiers; the social graph as identity.
- **4-2 [[Association-and-Plural-Publics]]** — End-to-end encrypted messaging, federated communication, secure multi-party computation, group-level privacy.
- **4-3 [[Commerce-and-Trust]]** — Quantitative trust networks; community currencies; alternatives to fungible universal money.
- **4-4 [[Property-and-Contract]]** — Digital asset sharing (storage, computation, data); usus / abusus / fructus; [[Data-Coalitions]]; smart contracts as incomplete artifacts of social norms.
- **4-5 [[Access]]** — The "digital divide"; right-to-access; digital public infrastructure (India Stack, Taiwan, Estonia's Diia/mRiik).

### Section 5: Democracy
A spectrum of "collaboration across diversity" technologies plotted on a depth-vs-breadth production-possibilities frontier.
- **5-0 [[Collaborative-Technology-and-Democracy]]** — How the book itself was made: Git, [[Quadratic-Funding]] via Gitcoin, advanced voting, the [[Plural-Management-Protocol]]. The depth/breadth spectrum (Community/State/Commodity).
- **5-1 [[Post-Symbolic-Communication]]** — Brain-to-brain interfaces, the Park of Aging at Miraikan, deeper-than-language sharing.
- **5-2 [[Immersive-Shared-Reality]]** — VR/AR/XR; risks of metaverse monopolization; embodied collaboration.
- **5-3 [[Creative-Collaborations]]** — Vesuvius Challenge, open-source codesign, generative AI as collaborator, "alien art".
- **5-4 [[Augmented-Deliberation]]** — Pol.is, Community Notes (Birdwatch), "broad listening", AI-assisted facilitation, citizen assemblies.
- **5-5 [[Adaptive-Administration]]** — Generative Foundation Models (GFMs) as administrative tools; the risks (opacity, regulatory arbitrage).
- **5-6 [[Plural-Voting]]** — [[Quadratic-Voting]], [[Liquid-Democracy]], eigenvoting/correlation discounting, predictive voting (Futarchy), assisted real-time voting.
- **5-7 [[Social-Markets]]** — [[Quadratic-Funding]] as the canonical Plural funding formula; [[Gitcoin]]; [[Data-Coalitions]]; reforming markets to internalize supermodularity.

### Section 6: Impact
Plural technology applied to specific sectors. (This wiki summarizes rather than creating a page per impact area.)
- **6-0 From Plurality to Reality** — How Plural tools change real institutions.
- **6-1 Workplace** — Co-ops, worker voice, multistakeholder governance.
- **6-2 Health** — Patient data coalitions, public-health bridging.
- **6-3 Media** — Bridging algorithms, decentralized journalism, Community Notes.
- **6-4 Environment** — Civic IoT, monitoring commons, environmental data.
- **6-5 Learning** — Open educational resources, peer learning, GFMs in education.

### Section 7: Forward
- **7-0 [[Policy]]** — The Taiwan model as synthesis of US, PRC, EU "digital empires" ([[Anu-Bradford]]); proposed 1% of GDP for [[Digital-Public-Infrastructure]]; lessons from [[ARPA]] / IPTO under [[JCR-Licklider|Licklider]]; new ⿻ regulation around [[Data-Coalitions]] and transnational digital ministries.
- **7-1 Conclusion** — Plurality as an infinite game; invitation to co-creation.

## How the book carves Plurality

- **Plurality is technology for collaboration across social difference** — not "diversity" in the narrow demographic sense, but the full range of overlapping affiliations.
- The book identifies a "[[Narrow-Corridor|narrow corridor]]" ([[Daron-Acemoglu|Acemoglu]] / [[James-Robinson|Robinson]]) between authoritarianism and social collapse, threatened from two sides by today's technology trends: anti-social erosion and centralizing [[Surveillance-Capitalism|surveillance]]/AI.
- Plurality is positioned as a "third way" between **[[Libertarianism]]** (atomist rights, no shared structure) and **[[Technocracy]]** (centralized optimization of a single welfare function) — both diagnosed as "[[Monist-Atomism|monist atomism]]".
- The Mandarin character pair 數位 captures the dual meaning "digital" / "plural" — central to the book's framing.
- Taiwan is presented as the leading real-world embodiment, with [[Audrey-Tang]] as its inaugural Minister of Digital Affairs.
