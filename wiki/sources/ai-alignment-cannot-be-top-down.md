## Summary
Tang argues that today's dominant approach to AI alignment — a handful of private corporations defining "aligned behaviour" behind closed doors — recapitulates the failed top-down moderation paradigm of 2010s social media. The remedy is *attentiveness*: structured citizen participation, open model specifications, market incentives that reward portability, and community-scale assistants tuned to local norms.

## Key claims

- Alignment cannot be defined in the abstract; it depends on context. Choices that prioritise (e.g.) freedom of expression are simultaneously choices that enable certain harms (e.g. fraud, disinformation).
- The Taiwan response to AI-generated scam ads (March 2024 — Jensen Huang deepfakes) shows that citizens can define alignment quickly and at scale: 200,000 random SMS → 447 demographically-representative citizens → cross-cluster *uncommon ground* → unanimous parliamentary support → by 2025, scam ads are gone.
- The current corporate-led approach is "deeply inattentive" and risks repeating Facebook's Rohingya disinformation failure. "Trying to answer all relevant questions through centralised, upstream programming inevitably failed under the weight of real-world complexity."
- The breakthrough on social media came with *outward shifts of power*: Twitter's Birdwatch → X's Community Notes — "auditable, distributed steering power."
- Three reinforcing levers: **industry norms** (public model specifications and clause-level reasoning citations — pointing to Anthropic's Constitution, OpenAI Model Spec, gpt-oss-safeguard); **market design** (Utah Digital Choice Act H.B. 418 mandates social-graph portability); **community-scale assistants** (Polis, RLCF — Reinforcement Learning from Community Feedback).
- "The real test is whether we can embed these practices into the everyday operation of AI. Give people the steering wheel. We, the people, are the alignment system we have been waiting for."
