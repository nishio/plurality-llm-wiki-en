## Summary

A reflection on Zuzalu (the 2023 popup-city experiment) and the 2024-2025 "network state" / charter-cities / new-cities ecosystem, proposing a distinction between **tribes** (culture-innovating communities with permanent *hubs*) and **zones** (jurisdictional / regulatory experiments). Buterin argues these are *complementary, not convergent* — they need different specialists — and proposes an "archipelago" model where tribes provide cultural depth and zones provide legal autonomy.

## Key claims

- **Zuzalu's lessons**: ~200 people (Dunbar's number) is the right size; 1-2 months is the right duration ("a week is a break from your life, two months *is* your life"); too much content overload is the most common popup failure mode.
- **Pattern of attenuation**: over time, popups got shorter, smaller and more generic — approaching mere conferences. Praxis "delivers parties in upscale US cities" rather than a Mediterranean renaissance.
- **The "two things core to the early ideology often fall away": novel governance designs and search for legal autonomy.** Forking-as-governance works fine at popup scale, masking the harder problem.
- **Critique of "universal culture"**: Scott Alexander's frame (markets surface the best ideas via individual choice) under-weights the immersion required for genuine cultural innovation. Three failure modes: top-down culture, traditionalism, individualism — mapped to the three corners of the *d/acc triangle*.
- **Charles Taylor on "social imaginaries"**: cultures are big complicated blobs where official statements and lived practice diverge, and the latter is decisive. Cited at length.
- **Tribes need permanent physical hubs** (Frontier Tower, Crecimiento, 4seas, ShanhaiWoo, Network School). Long-lasting space allows deep cultural immersion.
- **Zones, not new countries**, are the realistic vehicle for jurisdictional innovation. Liberland-style "ungoverned land" is rare and unstable; zones are palatable to governments and let host countries capture network upside (e.g. Prospera pays 12% of tax revenue to Honduras).
- **Three governance lineages within the zones space**: libertarians (corner freedom), developmentalists (Shenzhen as model), and *social technologists* (governance as social tech to iterate on). Buterin places himself in the third camp.
- **Explicit policy menu zones could try**:
  1. *Do urbanism right* (California Forever, Culdesac).
  2. *Let people in* — vouching as substitute for the "which country are you from" filter; tribes could provide collective visas.
  3. *Vouching (mandatory liability insurance) as general substitute for regulation* (Robin Hanson's idea, Prospera as the live experiment).
  4. *Crazy democracy ideas* — including Eliezer Yudkowsky's multi-level liquid democracy with 50-200-delegate tiers and pre-selected aristocracy resistance.
  5. *Crazy urban governance* — including Harberger taxes on land.
- **The deep critique of the Tech Right "country takeover" turn**: "instead of corporations and the state serving as a healthy check and balance against each other, the two collude against everyone else… ideas go straight from being twelve-thousand-word screeds to running entire countries." Tribes-and-zones is the *positive-sum* substitute.
- **Quote Fukuyama on liberalism**: "liberalism needs community, but it doesn't need a 'strong god'… liberalism's goal isn't to become this kind of community itself, but to be the platform where communities like this can grow up." Buterin endorses this, adds that the missing piece is *somebody has to actually build the tribes*.

## Significance for the wiki

This essay is the strongest explicit positioning of Buterin in the **Plurality** camp rather than the **Network State** camp. The book's [[Network-State]] page currently characterises Buterin as ambivalent (he reviewed Srinivasan's book somewhat sympathetically in 2022); this 2025 essay revises that — he is now explicitly anti-country-takeover and pro-pluralist-experimentation at sub-state scale, which is essentially the [[Plurality]] position with web3 vocabulary.

The essay also marks an evolution from the *Decentralized Society* framing (decentralised credentials and souls) to a *physical-spaces* framing (tribes need hubs, zones need land). The web3 vocabulary recedes; what remains is a Plurality-aligned commitment to "many small experiments, voluntary participation, no global takeovers."

## Open Questions

- Buterin uses the term "tribes" in a way that overlaps but does not fully coincide with Plurality's "intersecting publics" (Section 4-2). Tribes-as-Buterin-defines-them are *immersive* and *physical*; Plurality publics are *intersectional* and primarily *cognitive/communicative*. Are these the same primitive at different zoom levels, or different primitives?
- The d/acc-triangle reference is to Buterin's 2025-01-05 "d/acc one year later" post. If that post is ingestable, it would clarify how d/acc and Plurality fit together in his current synthesis.
