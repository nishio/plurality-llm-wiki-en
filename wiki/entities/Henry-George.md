## In the book

[[plurality-book]] names Henry George several times briefly, in chapters **2-1** and **3-0**:

- as "highly-influential author of the best-selling book on economics in American and perhaps world history, [who] made his career as a searing critic of private property in land";
- as a major influence on [[Sun-Yat-Sen]]'s Mínshēng principle ([[Tridemism]]) — "an American political economist known for his advocacy of land rights equality, anti-monopoly stances, and support for cooperative enterprises";
- as the philosophical mentor of [[John-Dewey]] (Dewey was "a disciple of Henry George who was visiting China partly to see how George's ideas might play out on a social scale");
- as the lineage behind Taiwan's **1977 Georgist land value tax** and the constitutional support for cooperative enterprise in Article 145 of the ROC Constitution;
- and in [[Connected-Society]], as part of the American tradition that informs [[Danielle-Allen]]'s thought.

## Background

Henry George (1839–1897) was an American political economist and journalist, best known for *Progress and Poverty* (1879) — for a time the best-selling book in the English language after the Bible. He proposed a **single tax** on the unimproved value of land as the just basis for public revenue, on the argument that land value is created by community presence and infrastructure, not by the landowner. His ideas — "Georgism" — directly influenced reformers from the Progressive Era through to contemporary land-value-tax advocates.
