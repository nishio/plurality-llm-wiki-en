## In the book

[[plurality-book]] cites Mark Granovetter briefly but at a key structural point in chapter **3-1 Living in a Plural World**:

> "Perhaps the crispest articulation of this vision appears in the work of the leading figure of network sociology, Mark Granovetter. There is no basic individual atom; personal identity fundamentally arises from social relationships and connections."

His 1985 paper *Economic Action and Social Structure: The Problem of Embeddedness* is footnoted. The book invokes him to establish that the **bidirectional equilibrium between diverse people and the social groups they form** is the essence of Plural social science — the descriptive counterpart of the natural-science shift from monism to complexity. [[Connected-Society]] is the chapter that builds on this.

## Background

Mark Granovetter (b. 1943) is an American sociologist, the Joan Butler Ford Professor at Stanford University. His paper "The Strength of Weak Ties" (1973) is among the most cited in the social sciences, founding modern social-network analysis. His work on **embeddedness** (1985) argued that economic action is embedded in concrete personal relationships and social networks, undermining both pure-market and pure-hierarchy analyses of the economy.
