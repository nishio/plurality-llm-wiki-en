## In the book

[[plurality-book]] names Doug Engelbart briefly in chapter **3-3 [[The-Lost-Dao]]**, as one of the three pioneer figures whose vision of networked, collaborative computing the book treats as the unfinished aspiration that [[Plurality]] inherits. He is identified by his 1968 **"Mother of All Demos"** — the public demonstration that introduced the mouse, hypertext, real-time text editing, video teleconferencing, and screen sharing.

The mention is one paragraph; the book's extended Lost Dao treatment focuses on [[JCR-Licklider]], with Engelbart and [[Ted-Nelson]] alongside.

## Background

Douglas Engelbart (1925–2013) was an American engineer and inventor. At SRI International's Augmentation Research Center he led the team that developed the computer mouse, hypertext, networked computing, and the concept of "augmenting human intellect". His 1968 presentation at the Fall Joint Computer Conference in San Francisco — the "Mother of All Demos" — is widely considered the most consequential single demo in the history of personal computing.
