## Description

From [[plurality-book]] chapter **2-2 The Life of a Digital Democracy**, vTaiwan is the consensus-finding platform that pairs the open-source **Pol.is** statistical opinion-mapping tool with structured deliberation processes. It is one of the canonical examples the book uses to illustrate [[Digital-Democracy]] and [[Augmented-Deliberation]] in practice.

## How it works

- Citizens submit statements about a policy question.
- Other participants vote agree / disagree / pass on each statement.
- Pol.is's machine-learning algorithm groups participants into opinion clusters and surfaces statements that earn cross-cluster agreement (the **bridging** statements).
- These bridging statements become the focal points of deliberation and inform government decisions.

## Notable cases

The book recounts vTaiwan's role in surfacing consensus on contested issues including the regulation of Uber and on policy around online sales of alcohol.

## Genealogy

vTaiwan grew out of the civic-tech community [[g0v]] in the wake of the [[Sunflower-Movement]], with [[Audrey-Tang]] as a key contributor before her transition into government.
