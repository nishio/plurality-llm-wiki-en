## Role in the book

From [[plurality-book]] chapter **3-0 What is Plurality?**, Danielle Allen supplies the **normative** pillar of the three-part definition of [[Plurality]]: the ideal of a Connected Society "where bridging ties, across lines of difference, are formed at a high rate" (Allen, "Towards a Connected Society", 2017).

Her book *Justice by Means of Democracy* (2023) is also cited in the **4-0 Rights** chapter for the argument that systems of rights are integral to democracy precisely because government cannot respond to "the will of the people" if that will cannot be safely expressed.

## Influence

The book associates the rainbow elements forming at the intersections of squares in its cover graphic with Allen's notion of [[Connected-Society]] — and notes that while Allen has given the clearest exposition, the underlying tradition draws on American thinkers like Henry George and John Dewey (themselves cited in the genealogy of [[Tridemism]] and Taiwan's [[Digital-Democracy]]).
