## Role in the book

J.C.R. Licklider (1915–1990), called "Lick" throughout [[plurality-book]], is the book's protagonist of [[The-Lost-Dao]] (chapter **3-3**). He is treated as the figure whose unfinished vision Plurality most directly inherits.

## Why he matters

- 1963 memo: **"Memorandum For Members and Affiliates of the Intergalactic Computer Network"** — the foundational document of the networked-computing vision.
- 1960s leadership of the **Information Processing Techniques Office (IPTO)** at [[ARPA]], where he funded the network of university research labs that became the modern internet.
- 1980 paper **"Computers and Government"** — Lick's late warning that the failure to sustain public investment would let private monopolies dominate digital life.
- 1948 work on auditory signal detection — the book uses Lick's mathematics to justify the square-root rule behind [[Quadratic-Voting]] (calling him, "deeply ironically", "one of the fathers of QV as well").

## Themes

- **Human–computer symbiosis**, not mere automation.
- **Computer as communication device**, not just a calculator.
- **Coopetitive** rather than competitive research labs as the right organizational form for public infrastructure.
- An understanding of the computer as a tool of association, identity, and commerce — anticipating what [[plurality-book]] articulates as the Plural rights/OS stack.

## Influence reading

The book argues Lick's failure was not technical but political — federal funding for his vision was not sustained — and that recovering his agenda requires a major increase in public investment in [[Digital-Public-Infrastructure]] today.
