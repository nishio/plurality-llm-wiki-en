## In the book

[[plurality-book]] mentions Neal Stephenson several times briefly but never devotes a sustained section to him. The book uses him to:

- Anchor the **antisocial / metaverse** dystopia: "In his 1992 classic, *Snow Crash*, Neal Stephenson imagines a future where most people have retreated to live much of their lives in an immersive 'metaverse'... Meta Platforms is named after Stephenson's metaverse." This frames the discussion of [[Immersive-Shared-Reality]] risk.
- Anchor the **Libertarianism canon**: "Fiction has been the central inspiration for libertarian thinking, including the work of Ayn Rand and Stephenson... Stephenson's books, especially *Snow Crash* and *Cryptonomicon* (1999), while seemingly and expressly intended as dystopian warnings, have been adopted as blueprints by adherents of Libertarianism."

The book treats his novels as cultural touchstones for both the antisocial-threat and the libertarian-blueprint sides of the technology debate.

## Background

Neal Stephenson (b. 1959) is an American writer, best known for the cyberpunk novel *Snow Crash* (1992) and the historical / cryptographic novel *Cryptonomicon* (1999), as well as the *Baroque Cycle* and *Anathem*. He coined the term "metaverse" in *Snow Crash*. He has worked as Chief Futurist at Magic Leap and remains an influential figure in technology culture.
