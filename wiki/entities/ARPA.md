## Description

ARPA (now DARPA) is the US Advanced Research Projects Agency. From [[plurality-book]] chapter **7-0 Policy**, ARPA's **Information Processing Techniques Office (IPTO)** under [[JCR-Licklider]] in the 1960s is treated as **"perhaps the most successful example of industrial policy in American and perhaps world history"**.

## Why it matters

The book argues IPTO under Lick demonstrated a distinctive model:

- seed-funding a network of university-based research labs (MIT, Stanford, UC Berkeley, Carnegie Tech / CMU, UCLA);
- pursuing a clear *vision* (human–computer symbiosis, attack-resilient networking, computer as communication device) rather than picking national-champion firms;
- selecting collaborators not for regional economic development but to maximize chances of realizing that vision;
- building a "coopetitive" — competitive and cooperative — research ecosystem.

The outputs included the modern internet and the world's leading computer-science departments.

## What went wrong

Per the book, the failure to **sustain** federal funding through the 1970s and beyond is what allowed the private monopolies of today (Microsoft, Apple, Google, Meta, Amazon) to fill the gap that ARPA had been building public infrastructure to occupy. This is the book's [[The-Lost-Dao]].

## What an updated ARPA-like model looks like

[[plurality-book]] argues the modern Plural [[Policy]] model needs to:

- shift from military-industrial to civil-society-led leadership (the book argues military hosting is no longer the right fit);
- operate transnationally rather than purely nationally;
- direct funding to open-source / Third Sector developers, not just academic research;
- be hosted by digital ministries rather than defense agencies.

Modern partial heirs include the India Stack, Taiwan's Plural Innovation, EU Gaia-X, and similar [[Digital-Public-Infrastructure]] initiatives.
