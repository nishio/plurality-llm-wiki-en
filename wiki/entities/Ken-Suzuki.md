## Role

Ken Suzuki is, as of the 2024-2026 upstream additions to [[plurality-book]], **a named precursor of Plurality in the English manuscript**. He appears in three places:

1. as an **endorser** in `0-0-endorsements.md` ([[pluralitybook-upstream-2024-2026]] PR #918),
2. as the originator of **"Nameraka Society"** in the new sub-section of chapter 3-3 [[The-Lost-Dao]] (PR #1055),
3. as the originator of **dividual democracy / divicracy** in a 5-6 [[Plural-Voting]] footnote (PR #1050),
4. as the originator of **PICSY (Propagational Investment Currency SYstem)** in a 5-7 [[Social-Markets]] footnote (PR #1051).

## Biographical sketch (from the book's footnotes)

- Co-founder and Executive Chair at SmartNews, Inc. (endorsement byline, PR #918).
- Researcher in complex systems and artificial life (3-3 sub-section, PR #1055).
- Author of *The Nameraka Society and its Enemies* (なめらかな社会とその敵), Keiso Shobo Publishing, 2013 — the Japanese book that consolidates his 2000s vision (PR #1055).
- Author of *Propagational Investment Currency System (PICSY): proposing a new currency system using social computing*, PhD diss., Tokyo University, 2009 (PR #1051).

## What Suzuki contributes to the Plurality manuscript

### A second cybernetics lineage parallel to ARPA / IPTO

The PR #1055 sub-section "Quality Control and Nameraka Society" reframes Norbert Wiener's cybernetics as flowing **two ways out of MIT**: westward into the ARPA / IPTO / internet lineage that the book's main [[The-Lost-Dao|3-3]] tells, and **eastward into Japanese industrial organization** via W. Edwards Deming (a Teleological Society colleague of Wiener), the **Deming Prize** (JUSE, 1951), Toyota's PDCA / Kaizen, and from there into Stuart Kauffman's complex-systems work and Suzuki's Nameraka Society. The book now treats this as **"another lost Dao"**: cybernetic feedback applied not to packet-switched networks but to manufacturing → society.

### Nameraka ("smooth") Society

See dedicated concept page [[Nameraka-Society]]. The Nameraka vision treats society as a complex web-like network in which the rigid scale-free patterns of **membrane (inside/outside)** and **nucleus (control via few-DOF parameters)** — e.g. nation-state citizenship and state power — should be dissolved by technology so individuals can live as **dividuals** (分人) participating in multiple communities simultaneously. The book pairs this with Carl Schmitt's friend/enemy distinction (which Nameraka tries to overcome via smooth technological management of violence).

### Constructive Social Contract Theory (~2005)

The book's 3-3 sub-section credits Suzuki with proposing **automatically executable, human-and-machine-readable legal language** around 2005, "preceded the 2014 invention of Ethereum, which built a foundation for social contracts based on automatically executing smart contracts with blockchain." Read alongside [[Property-and-Contract]] this is a Plurality-internal claim of priority for the social-contract-as-code idea.

### Divicracy

PR #1050's [[Liquid-Democracy]] footnote (now `[^Divicracy]` rather than `[^PICSY]`): dividual democracy a.k.a. divicracy "includes not only delegation of one's vote to others but also splitting one's vote to multiple political issues." Cited to Suzuki, *The Nameraka Society and its Enemies* (2013). Suzuki "introduced the concept in 2000s and elaborated [it] in his book in 2013."

### PICSY

PR #1051's [[Social-Markets]] footnote attaches Suzuki's PICSY to the **inter-temporal economic system** bullet of "[[Quadratic-Funding|⿻ funding] across boundaries". Definition: "a value propagation system which tracks past transactions as contributions and assigns a portion of recent contributions to past contributors. Thus, in the framework of PICSY, transactions are track records for investments." This is the book's nominated mechanism for intergenerational matching funds / "ministry for the future".

### Endorsement (PR #918)

> "Plurality is a social philosophy of the technological era presented by Audrey Tang and Glen Weyl as a third way beyond Libertarianism and Technocracy. Its essence lies in the emergence of life at the edge of chaos, and whether this ideal is realized depends on whether the readers of this book become true activists who leap into that edge."

This is one of the few endorsements in `0-0-endorsements.md` that articulates a **theoretical reading** of Plurality (third way beyond [[Libertarianism]] and [[Technocracy]], life at the edge of chaos) rather than a generic public approval.
