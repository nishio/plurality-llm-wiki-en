## In the book

[[plurality-book]] mentions Charles Dodgson briefly in chapter **5-6 Plural Voting**, as the figure who in *The Principles of Parliamentary Representation* (1884) first studied the question of weighting votes for people holding multiple votes — "the same line of thinking that later led to [[Quadratic-Voting]]" and to [[Liquid-Democracy]]. The book notes parenthetically that he is "the mathematician better known as Lewis Carroll".

## Background

Charles Lutwidge Dodgson (1832–1898) — best known by his pen name **Lewis Carroll** — was an Oxford mathematician, logician, and Anglican deacon. He published *Alice's Adventures in Wonderland* (1865) and *Through the Looking-Glass* (1871). His professional mathematical work included several pamphlets on voting theory and proportional representation (1873, 1884), now recognized as early contributions to social-choice theory predating Arrow.
