## Role in the book

From [[plurality-book]] chapter **3-0 What is Plurality?**, Hannah Arendt (1906–1975) supplies the **descriptive** pillar of the three-part definition of [[Plurality]]:

> "Action, the only activity that goes on directly between men without the intermediary of things or matter, corresponds to the human condition of plurality, to the fact that men, not Man, live on the earth and inhabit the world." — *The Human Condition*, 1958

The book identifies Plurality at the descriptive level — "the social world is neither an unorganized collection of isolated individuals nor a monolithic whole" — explicitly with Arendt's framing.

## Influence

Beyond the chapter epigraph, the book treats Arendt as the grounding philosophical reference for the idea that the *fact* of human plurality precedes and conditions any political or technical project. The book's pluralism stands against what it calls "monist atomism", and Arendt's *Human Condition* is the central philosophical source for the alternative.
