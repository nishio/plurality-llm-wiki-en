## Background

Joan C. Tronto is an American political theorist (University of Minnesota), best known for the political/feminist ethics of care developed in *Moral Boundaries: A Political Argument for an Ethic of Care* (1993) and extended in *Caring Democracy: Markets, Equality, and Justice* (2013).

## In the wiki

Tronto enters this wiki through [[Audrey-Tang]]'s post-book Civic AI work. From [[civic-ai-manifesto]] and [[Civic-AI]], Tronto's four phases of care — *caring about, taking care of, care-giving, care-receiving* — become the first four packs of Tang and Caroline Green's 6-Pack of Care. *Caring Democracy* contributes the fifth pack ("caring with" = solidarity). The sixth pack (symbiosis / Kami of care) is Tang and Green's own addition.

Tang quotes Tronto's foundational argument from *Moral Boundaries*: "care was excluded from serious moral and political consideration by historically constructed boundaries — between morality and politics, public and private life, and a 'moral point of view' that prizes detachment over responsiveness." Tang argues that current AI governance reproduces the same pattern by "constructing 'alignment' and 'safety' as purely technical categories" that exclude relational concerns before the conversation begins.

## Why Tang invokes care ethics

From [[civic-ai-manifesto]]:
- Consequentialism fails when AI optimises 10,000× faster than human oversight can predict outcomes.
- Deontology fails when interpreters operate at vastly mismatched speeds.
- Care ethics "starts from relationships and process rather than from outcomes or rules alone."

Tronto also provides the structural argument that care is only viable as a political ideal "when liberal, pluralistic, democratic institutions already guarantee the rights and justice it depends on" — Tang treats this as the link between [[Plurality]] and Civic AI.
