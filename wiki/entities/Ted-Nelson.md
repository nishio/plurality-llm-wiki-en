## In the book

[[plurality-book]] mentions Ted Nelson briefly in chapter **3-3 [[The-Lost-Dao]]**, alongside [[JCR-Licklider]] and [[Doug-Engelbart]]. The book credits him with **Project Xanadu**, two-way bidirectional hyperlinks, transclusion, and micropayments to creators — features the contemporary web famously lacks. He is one of the three figures whose unfinished early-internet vision the book treats as the Plural Dao that was lost when private platforms took over.

## Background

Ted Nelson (b. 1937) is an American information-technology pioneer who coined the terms "hypertext", "hypermedia", and "transclusion" in 1963. He founded Project Xanadu in 1960 — the longest-running unfinished project in computing — which envisioned a richer hyperlink system than the one-way links eventually adopted by the World Wide Web. His books include *Computer Lib / Dream Machines* (1974) and *Literary Machines* (1981).
