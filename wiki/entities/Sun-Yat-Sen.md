## In the book

[[plurality-book]] introduces Sun Yat-Sen in chapter **2-1 A View From Yushan**, in the Tridemism section. The book describes him as "a young, American-educated, Christian doctor and activist" who, similarly inspired by Qing's defeat at Japan's hands, articulated the **Three Principles of the People** in 1905 — the [[Tridemism]] that became the official philosophy of the ROC. The book notes his exile in Japan, his "Revive China Society", his founding of the KMT, and his death in 1925.

While Sun is a load-bearing figure in the Tridemism chapter, the engagement is essentially biographical and historical — the book uses him as the named source of Tridemism rather than analyzing his writings in depth.

## Background

Sun Yat-Sen (孫中山, 1866–1925) was a Chinese revolutionary and political leader. He served as the first provisional president of the Republic of China in 1912 and as the founding father of the Kuomintang (KMT, Chinese Nationalist Party). His [[Tridemism]] — Mínzú (民族), Mínquán (民權), Mínshēng (民生) — fused Western democratic ideas (drawn from Lincoln and [[Henry-George]] among others) with Confucian and Chinese reformist traditions. He is uniquely revered in both the PRC ("Forerunner of the Revolution") and the ROC ("Father of the Nation").
