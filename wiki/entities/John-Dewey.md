## In the book

[[plurality-book]] mentions John Dewey briefly but in load-bearing places:

- in chapter **2-1 A View From Yushan**, as "perhaps the most respected American philosopher and among the most respected educators and philosophers of democracy globally" — a "disciple of [[Henry-George]]" who visited China in 1919, met [[Sun-Yat-Sen]], gave more than 200 lectures, and whose pragmatic / experimentalist theory of democracy, translated as "experimentalism" by his Chinese student [[Hu-Shih]], "resonated with the uncertain and exploratory atmosphere of early ROC";
- the [[Connected-Society]] chapter (**3-2**) returns to him: "John Dewey, widely considered the greatest philosopher of American democracy, argued that the standard national and state institutions that instantiated the idea hardly scratched the surface of what democracy required." A long Dewey quotation from *The Public and its Problems* (1927) opens the chapter.

Dewey is treated as one of the American thinkers whose ideas, via Taiwan, feed into the [[Plurality]] project — but no chapter is devoted to him.

## Background

John Dewey (1859–1952) was an American philosopher, psychologist and educational reformer, a founder of pragmatism alongside William James and Charles Sanders Peirce. His major works include *Democracy and Education* (1916), *Experience and Nature* (1925), and *The Public and its Problems* (1927). He spent 1919–1921 in China and Japan, where his ideas significantly shaped the New Culture Movement.
