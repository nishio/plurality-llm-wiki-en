## In the book

[[plurality-book]] mentions Georg Simmel in a single sentence in chapter **3-2 Connected Society**: "Georg Simmel, one of the founders of sociology, originated the idea of the 'web' as a critique of the individualist concept of identity." He is listed alongside [[John-Dewey]] and [[Norbert-Wiener]] as part of the pre-history of the [[Connected-Society]] / [[Plurality]] view.

## Background

Georg Simmel (1858–1918) was a German sociologist, philosopher, and critic, one of the founding generation of sociology alongside Max Weber, Emile Durkheim, and Ferdinand Tönnies. His most influential contributions include analyses of money, urban life, and the **"web of group affiliations"** (*Die Kreuzung sozialer Kreise*, 1908) — the idea that personal identity emerges from the unique intersection of group memberships a person holds, anticipating intersectionality and network sociology.
