## In the book

[[plurality-book]] cites Daron Acemoglu briefly but in load-bearing places:

- in chapter **2-0**, his co-authored book *The Narrow Corridor: States, Societies, and the Fate of Liberty* (with [[James-Robinson]], 2020) is the source of the **"narrow corridor"** metaphor between social collapse and authoritarianism that the book uses to frame technology's twin threats to democracy;
- in the discussion of Science and Technology Studies, *Power and Progress* (with Simon Johnson, 2023) is cited as evidence "that the direction of technological progress is a key target for social policy and reform" — undercutting the assumption that the path of technology is predetermined.

He is referenced as a supporting authority rather than discussed at length.

## Background

Daron Acemoglu (b. 1967) is a Turkish-American economist and Institute Professor at MIT. He won the 2024 Nobel Memorial Prize in Economic Sciences (jointly with Simon Johnson and [[James-Robinson]]) for work on how institutions shape economic prosperity. He is the author or co-author of *Why Nations Fail*, *The Narrow Corridor*, and *Power and Progress*.
