## In the book

[[plurality-book]] names Norbert Wiener only briefly, in chapter **3-2 Connected Society**:

> "Norbert Wiener coined the term 'cybernetics' for a field studying such rich interactive systems. By perceiving the limits of the box of modernity they highlighted even as they helped construct it, these pioneers helped us imagine a social world outside it, pointing the way towards a vision of a Connected Society that harnesses the potential of collaboration across diversity."

He appears in a list alongside [[Georg-Simmel]] (the "web" critique of individualist identity) and [[John-Dewey]] (American democracy's limits) as a pre-history of the Plural / [[Connected-Society]] vision. The mention is one sentence.

## Background

Norbert Wiener (1894–1964) was an American mathematician and philosopher, Professor of Mathematics at MIT. He coined the term **[[Cybernetics]]** in 1948 — the study of control and communication in animals, machines and society. His foundational text *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine* (1948) and the popular *The Human Use of Human Beings* (1950) established a transdisciplinary framework that influenced early computer science, ecology, neuroscience, and management theory.
