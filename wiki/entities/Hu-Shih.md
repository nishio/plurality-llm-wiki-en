## In the book

[[plurality-book]] mentions Hu Shih briefly in chapter **2-1**:

- as **[[John-Dewey]]'s Chinese student**, who translated Dewey's "pragmatic" theory of democracy as **"experimentalism"** — the term carrying the methodological / exploratory flavor the book argues resonated with the early ROC;
- as the figure who, after fleeing alongside the KMT to Taiwan, served as **President of [[Academia-Sinica]]** and became "a central influence on the development of Taiwan's educational system", fusing Confucian tradition with Deweyan pragmatism, egalitarianism and democracy.

He functions as the conduit through which the American pragmatist tradition entered the cultural foundation of Taiwan's later [[Digital-Democracy]].

## Background

Hu Shih (胡適, 1891–1962) was a Chinese philosopher, essayist, and diplomat. A student of [[John-Dewey]] at Columbia, he was a central figure of the New Culture Movement and of the May Fourth Movement in 1919, advocating literary vernacular and democratic reform. He served as the Republic of China's ambassador to the United States (1938–1942) and as the second President of Academia Sinica (1958–1962) in Taiwan.
