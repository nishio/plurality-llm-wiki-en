## In the book

[[plurality-book]] mentions Chiang Kai-Shek briefly in chapter **2-1 A View From Yushan**, in the Postbellum-Taiwan section:

- as the dictator who, defeated by the Communists, relocated the ROC government to Taiwan in 1949 and imposed martial law (the "White Terror") on the native population;
- as the figure who, lacking ties to the island's local elites, was nonetheless able to impose the **Rural Land Reform** (1949 rent reduction to 37.5%, 1951 release of public land, 1953 "land to the tiller" policy) and the constitutional protection for cooperative enterprise (Article 145) — both legacies of [[Tridemism]]'s Mínshēng principle that the book credits with laying Taiwan's egalitarian economic foundation;
- briefly, in the lineage of his son Chiang Ching-Kuo, whose 1980s liberalization opened the door to direct elections and the democratic movement.

The book's treatment is institutional and historical; it does not engage with Chiang's broader politics in depth.

## Background

Chiang Kai-Shek (蔣介石, 1887–1975) was a Chinese Nationalist political and military leader. He led the Kuomintang (KMT) from the death of [[Sun-Yat-Sen]] (1925) until his own death in 1975 and served as President of the Republic of China both on the mainland (1928–1949) and in Taiwan (1950–1975). His relocation to Taiwan in 1949 with around two million ROC soldiers and civilians began the modern history of the ROC on Taiwan.
