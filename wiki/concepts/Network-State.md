## In the book

[[plurality-book]] mentions the Network State only briefly in chapter **2-0**: *The Network State* (2022) by [[Balaji-Srinivasan]] is listed as a canonical text of contemporary [[Libertarianism]], alongside *The Sovereign Individual*, [[Curtis-Yarvin]]'s *Unqualified Reservations* (as "Mencius Moldbug"), and *Bronze Age Mindset*. The book treats it as one expression of the worldview that cryptography, "exit" and floating sovereignties can supplant deliberative collective politics.

The book does not engage with the Network State proposal on its own terms; it appears only as evidence of the Libertarian intellectual canon that [[Plurality]] is offered as an alternative to.

## Background

*The Network State: How to Start a New Country* (self-published, 2022) is [[Balaji-Srinivasan]]'s blueprint for building new states by first gathering an internet-native community around a shared moral mission, then crowdfunding territory, then negotiating diplomatic recognition. The book has been a touchstone of the broader cryptocurrency / crypto-state movement and a frequent reference in seasteading, charter-city, and "exit" discussions.

## Updates

### 2026-05-28 — Vitalik Buterin's "tribes-and-zones" as explicit distancing

From [[vitalik-let-a-thousand-societies-bloom]] (Dec 2025): [[Vitalik-Buterin]] — who reviewed *The Network State* somewhat sympathetically in 2022 — now explicitly distances himself from the country-takeover wing of the network-state movement. He proposes **zones** (jurisdictional experiments hosted by sovereign countries, e.g. Bhutan's Gelephu Mindfulness City, Prospera in Honduras, California Forever) as a *substitute* for new countries, and **tribes** (cultural communities with permanent hubs) as a substitute for political consolidation.

The two-paragraph anti-takeover passage is sharp: "instead of corporations and the state serving as a healthy check and balance against each other, the two collude against everyone else… ideas go straight from being twelve-thousand-word screeds or five hour podcasts to running entire countries." Buterin's empirical claim is that people who start working on tribes and zones "suddenly get visibly *more* positive-sum and *less* interested in country takeovers."

This is a substantial revision to the book's chapter 2-0 framing of *Network State* as canonical Libertarianism: by late 2025, one of the book's own co-credited contributors has publicly repudiated the *country-creation* part of network-state ideology while keeping the *experimentation* part — which is essentially the [[Plurality]] position. The wiki should record this as a genuine evolution of the web3 → Plurality bridge.
