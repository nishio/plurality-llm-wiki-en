## In the book

[[plurality-book]] discusses Futarchy briefly in chapter **5-6 [[Plural-Voting]]** as one of the modes of "predictive voting" within the Plural Voting family. The book summarizes it as **[[Robin-Hanson]]'s proposal: "vote on values, bet on beliefs in prediction markets"** — citizens vote on the welfare metric they want to maximize, and policy choices are then evaluated through markets that predict each policy's effect on that metric.

The [[plurality-book]] also reports using a **hybrid futarchy-like mechanism** in its own production process: contributors simultaneously vote on proposed changes to the text and predict the outcomes (whether the change will be retained, etc.). This is one of the live demonstrations of the [[Plural-Management-Protocol]].

## How it relates to other Plural Voting modes

Where [[Quadratic-Voting]] aggregates **strength of preference** and [[Liquid-Democracy]] aggregates **trust through delegation**, Futarchy separates the **value question** (what do we want?) from the **factual prediction** (what works?). The book groups all three under the broader claim that the one-person-one-vote ballot is too thin a signal for the complexity of Plural societies.
