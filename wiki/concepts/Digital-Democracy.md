## Definition

From [[plurality-book]], Digital Democracy is the project of treating democracy and digital technology not as adversaries but as **deeply mutually entwined allies** — democracy reimagined at internet scale, supported by digital tools that match the richness of social life rather than reducing it to atomized individual choice.

The book argues that the present "low tide" of democracy is the product of technological directions liberal democracies have collectively chosen to invest in (foundation models for centralized surveillance, social media that polarizes). Reversing course requires deliberately funding and building democratic technology.

## Taiwan as the canonical example

[[plurality-book]]'s **2-1 A View From Yushan** and **2-2 The Life of a Digital Democracy** present Taiwan as the place that has "gone farther than any other in realizing that future". Key practices the book cites:

- The 2014 [[Sunflower-Movement]] occupation of the legislature and the appointment of "reverse mentors" from civil society — including the future Minister of Digital Affairs [[Audrey-Tang]].
- The civic-tech community [[g0v]] and its consensus-finding platform [[vTaiwan]].
- The government petition platform [[Join]].
- Pol.is-based bridging deliberation surfacing rough consensus across cleavages.
- The COVID-19 mask-distribution map built by civic developers on government open data.
- Sustained voter turnout above 70%, low pandemic fatality without lockdown, and resilient information environment in the face of foreign disinformation.

## Distinct from "democracy + internet"

The book is careful that Digital Democracy is **not** just porting existing voting and representative institutions online. It is a deeper redesign that draws on:

- new voting methods ([[Quadratic-Voting]], [[Liquid-Democracy]], approval voting),
- new funding mechanisms ([[Quadratic-Funding]]),
- new deliberation tools ([[Augmented-Deliberation]]),
- and new infrastructure ([[Digital-Public-Infrastructure]], [[Identity-and-Personhood]] systems).

## Genealogy

From [[plurality-book]], Taiwan's particular form of Digital Democracy synthesizes:

- [[Tridemism]] ([[Sun-Yat-Sen]]'s Three Principles of the People), especially ethnic pluralism and civil livelihood;
- [[John-Dewey]]'s pragmatist experimentalism (transmitted via [[Hu-Shih]]);
- [[Henry-George]]'s land-value taxation (the 1977 land-value tax in Taiwan);
- post-war rural land reform and a strong cooperative "Third Sector";
- and a civic-tech tradition rising from the [[Sunflower-Movement]].
