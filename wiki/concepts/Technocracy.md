## In the book

[[plurality-book]] uses "Technocracy" — borrowed from the *Civilization VI* "Synthetic Technocracy" ideology choice — as one of two foil ideologies that [[Plurality]] positions itself against. (The other is [[Libertarianism]].) In chapter **2-0** the book lays out the Technocratic vision:

- centered on AI / "Artificial General Intelligence" ([[AGI]]) as the engine of a coming transformation;
- aimed at "[[Sam-Altman|Moore's Law for Everything]]" — material abundance enabled by AI, distributed through mechanisms like [[Universal-Basic-Income]];
- requiring strong, often centralized control over who has access to powerful AI systems, in order to keep them aligned with human desires.

The book names [[Sam-Altman]], his mentor [[Reid-Hoffman]], and [[Elon-Musk]] as leading Silicon Valley exponents; [[Andrew-Yang]] as the political mainstreamer in the US; and authors like [[Ray-Kurzweil]], [[Nick-Bostrom]], [[Isaac-Asimov]] and [[Iain-M-Banks]] as cultural / fictional sources. PRC commentators Jack Ma and Yu Yong-Ding are listed as PRC-side exponents, and the PRC's New Generation AI Development Plan is identified as a Technocratic state strategy.

## Why it is a foil for Plurality

[[plurality-book]] groups Technocracy with [[Libertarianism]] under the umbrella of **[[Monist-Atomism]]**: both treat the world as made of atoms (whether individuals or a unified social whole) governed by a single set of laws. Technocracy's specific failing, per the book: by aiming to **replace** social messiness with a unified rational planner, it papers over (or erases) the diversity and intersectional structure that Plural science sees as the basic stuff of human society.
