## Definition

Plurality is defined in [[plurality-book]] as **"technology for collaboration across social difference"**. The book draws the word from the original *Star Trek* Vulcan philosophy of "Infinite Diversity in Infinite Combinations", and from the Mandarin pair 數位 (*shùwèi* / *shuwei*), which in Taiwan simultaneously means **"plural"** when applied to people and **"digital"** when applied to technology. The book uses a Unicode glyph as shorthand for the concept; this wiki spells it out as "Plurality" or "Plural" depending on context.

## Three-part definition

From [[plurality-book]], Plurality has three tightly connected components, each associated with one thinker:

1. **Descriptive — [[Hannah-Arendt]]**: the social world is neither isolated individuals nor a monolithic whole but a fabric of diverse and intersecting affiliations that constitute both personal identity and collective organization. (Arendt, *The Human Condition*, 1958.) See [[Connected-Society]].
2. **Normative — [[Danielle-Allen]]**: diversity is the fuel of social progress; societies succeed to the extent they harness it rather than letting it explode into conflict. (Allen, "Towards a Connected Society", 2017.)
3. **Prescriptive — [[Audrey-Tang]]**: digital technology should aspire to build the engines that harness and avoid conflagration of diversity, much as industrial technology contained the explosions of physical fuel.

## Contrast with Libertarianism and Technocracy

A core move in [[plurality-book]] is to position Plurality against what it calls **monist atomism** — the shared root of both Libertarianism (atomistic individuals defended by immutable rights) and Technocracy (centralized optimization of a single welfare function). Plurality instead foregrounds intersecting groups, multi-scale organization, and emergence — analogous to the shift in twentieth-century science from reductionism to complexity, networks, and ecosystems.

## Why "Digital" and "Plural" together

In [[plurality-book]] the conflation 數位 = digital = plural is more than a pun. It signals that:

- digital technology is *intrinsically* about representing and connecting diverse social groups (a hypergraph of people, groups and digital assets, see [[Identity-and-Personhood]]);
- the substantive aspiration of digital technology should be Plurality — collaboration across difference — not surveillance, monopoly, or atomization.

[[Audrey-Tang]] became the inaugural Minister of Digital Affairs (數位部) of Taiwan in 2022 — the book reads her title itself as embodying this fusion.
