## In the book

[[plurality-book]] discusses AGI primarily in chapter **2-0**, where it is identified as **central to the [[Technocracy]] vision**:

> "While the precise contours differ across the exponents of this view, the idea of 'Artificial General Intelligence' (AGI) is central: machines that exceed human capabilities in some generalized way, leaving little measurable utility in human individual or collective cognition."

The book is critical of the term in two ways:

1. **Mystique problem** — the book deliberately prefers **[[Generative-Foundation-Models]]** (GFMs) throughout, arguing "AGI" / "AI" carries excessive mystique and a misleading implication of autonomy.
2. **Worldview problem** — AGI-centered futurism (associated with [[Sam-Altman]], [[Reid-Hoffman]], [[Elon-Musk]], [[Ray-Kurzweil]], [[Nick-Bostrom]] and the science-fiction tradition of [[Isaac-Asimov]] and [[Iain-M-Banks]]) tends to concentrate decision-making power in a small group of engineers and to treat human cognition as something to be made obsolete rather than augmented.

The book argues that Plural deployment of these models — with [[Data-Coalitions]], [[Augmented-Deliberation]], [[Plural-Voting]] for governance, and back-propagation of value to data creators — is the alternative to the AGI / centralized-superintelligence frame.
