## Definition

From [[plurality-book]] chapter **5-1 Post-Symbolic Communication**, this is the speculative far end of the depth-vs-breadth spectrum: communication that bypasses symbolic mediation (language, written symbol, visual representation) and shares experience more directly. The chapter discusses **brain-to-brain interfaces** drawing on Rajesh Rao et al.'s 2014 "Direct Brain-to-Brain Interface in Humans".

The chapter opens with the Park of Aging at Miraikan as a kind of immersive empathy machine that gestures at the idea of "feeling what another feels".

## Justification

Even taking brain-to-brain interfaces as "wildly futuristic and disturbingly invasive", the book argues they pass the Plural test of "cooperation across difference": their key aspiration is to let children retain more of their imagination as they grow, by sharing it directly with adults — and to make profound understanding possible across vast experiential gaps that language cannot bridge.

## Trade-offs

The book pairs Post-Symbolic Communication with **approval voting** (one of the simplest [[Plural-Voting]] mechanisms) as the two extremes of Plural collaboration: the deepest and the broadest. Approval voting can cross hundreds of millions; brain-to-brain interfaces may reach only a handful but at unprecedented depth.

The chapter explicitly raises the dystopian counter: post-symbolic sharing could be used to manipulate. As with [[Immersive-Shared-Reality]], the book argues the risk is mitigated only by reading the technology alongside the full Plural stack.

## Open Questions

- The chapter is the most speculative in the book; no near-term policy implications follow.
- Whether "post-symbolic" is a meaningful category (since brain-to-brain interfaces themselves require symbolic encoding) is left open.

## Updates

### 2026-05-27: Clancy / Lowry footnotes added upstream (PR #1061)

From [[pluralitybook-upstream-2024-2026]] (PR #1061, kronosapiens, merged 2025-02-19): chapter 5-1 now carries two new pop-sci-flavoured footnotes:

- A **`[^Clancy]` footnote on romantic intimacy** citing Kelly Clancy's 2024 *Playing With Reality* — Clancy speculates that *Homo sapiens*' large pre-frontal cortex evolved not (as in the social-brain hypothesis) for managing many social relationships but rather for managing **a single highly complex relationship with a romantic partner**. The book treats wider social use of the larger brain as a "happy accident."
- A **`[^Lowry]` footnote on cross-generational experience-sharing** citing Lois Lowry's 1993 young adult novel *The Giver* — used to illustrate the "frontier" possibility of children learning from ancestors' lived experiences via post-symbolic channels.

These are minor but stylistically notable: the chapter's references widen from neuroscience / VR engineering to include popular-science speculation and young-adult fiction, which fits its already speculative tone.
