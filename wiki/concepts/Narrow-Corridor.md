## In the book

[[plurality-book]] adopts the "narrow corridor" metaphor in chapter **2-0** as a foundational framing: "As [[Daron-Acemoglu]] and [[James-Robinson]] famously argued, free democratic societies exist in a 'narrow corridor' between social collapse and authoritarianism. From both sides, information technologies seem to be narrowing the corridor, squeezing the possibility of a free society."

The book then organizes its diagnosis of the present along the two walls of the corridor:

- **Anti-social** technological threats (social media, atomizing cryptography, financialization) push toward **collapse** of the social fabric;
- **Centralizing** technological threats (machine learning, foundation models, IoT surveillance) push toward **authoritarian** control.

[[Plurality]] is then offered as a third way that widens the corridor by building Plural technologies that strengthen civil society without concentrating control.

The metaphor recurs in chapter **3-1**, where complexity-science notions of the "edge of chaos" and "self-organizing criticality" are mapped onto the same narrow-corridor idea.

## Background

The "narrow corridor" is the central image of [[Daron-Acemoglu]] and [[James-Robinson]]'s *The Narrow Corridor: States, Societies, and the Fate of Liberty* (Penguin, 2019). The argument: liberty depends on a balanced contest between a strong state and a strong society; when either dominates, liberty collapses (into despotism or into "absent leviathan" chaos). The corridor is path-dependent and hard to re-enter once exited.
