## Origin in the book

"The Lost Dao" is the title of chapter **3-3** of [[plurality-book]]. It is the book's name for the **early internet vision that pointed at Plurality but was abandoned** as the network commercialized.

## The figures

[[plurality-book]] centers the chapter on three figures:

- **[[JCR-Licklider]]** (Lick) — wrote the 1963 "Memorandum For Members and Affiliates of the Intergalactic Computer Network" and led the **Information Processing Techniques Office (IPTO)** at ARPA, funding the research network that became the internet. The book treats Lick as its protagonist of "what could have been".
- **[[Doug-Engelbart]]** — pioneered networked collaborative computing (the 1968 "Mother of All Demos").
- **[[Ted-Nelson]]** — Project Xanadu, two-way bidirectional hyperlinks, transclusion, micropayments to creators.

## What was lost

According to [[plurality-book]], Licklider's ARPA-funded vision included:

- the computer as a **communication device**, not just a calculator;
- **networked OSs** that would weave together rights, identity, association, commerce, and property — what later became [[Identity-and-Personhood]], [[Association-and-Plural-Publics]], [[Commerce-and-Trust]], [[Property-and-Contract]].
- public infrastructure mission, not narrow industrial-policy "national champions".

By the 1980s — as Lick himself warned in his 1980 paper "Computers and Government" — federal investment was withdrawn, and private corporate platforms (the book lists Microsoft, Apple, Google, Meta, Amazon) filled the resulting vacuum, building closed proprietary stacks where Lick had imagined an open Plural commons.

## What is missing today

The book uses The Lost Dao to enumerate the "missing layers" of the internet that current Plural projects are trying to restore:

- a widely adopted non-proprietary identity protocol (decentralized identifiers / DIDs);
- non-proprietary protocols for free association and messaging (MIMI, MLS, DIDComm);
- non-proprietary payment and commerce protocols;
- protocols for sharing computation, storage and data (Filecoin/IPFS, Holochain).

It also flags the **web3 / decentralized web** ecosystems, India's **India Stack**, and Europe's **Gaia-X** as partial proofs of concept that more systematic [[Digital-Public-Infrastructure]] is feasible.

## Why "Dao"

The chapter name plays on the Mandarin Daoist tradition that the book argues was congenial to [[John-Dewey]]'s pragmatism and to the Taiwanese democratic tradition: a "way" that is processual, experimental, and emerges from collaboration across difference rather than from imposed law.
