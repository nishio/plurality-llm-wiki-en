## Definition

From [[plurality-book]] chapter **2-1 A View From Yushan**, the Sunflower Movement (太陽花學運, "318" after its starting date) was the March 18, 2014 occupation of Taiwan's legislature by students protesting a trade deal with Beijing. The occupation lasted more than three weeks and ended with the protesters' demands largely accepted as consensus.

## Significance for Digital Democracy

The book treats the movement as a pivot point:

- it led to a change of government and the rise of new political parties;
- the government invited younger "reverse mentors" from civil society to help officials learn from youth — including [[Jaclyn-Tsai]], one of the world's first ministers in charge of digital participation;
- [[Jaclyn-Tsai|Tsai]] recruited [[Audrey-Tang]], who ultimately took the role in 2016 and in 2022 became Taiwan's first Minister of Digital Affairs;
- the technologies developed in and after the movement — including civic-tech tools like [[vTaiwan]] — became part of Taiwan's distinctive [[Digital-Democracy]].

## Comparison the book draws

[[plurality-book]] explicitly contrasts the Sunflower occupation with the January 6, 2021 occupation of the US Capitol: the US event lasted hours and remains divisive; Sunflower lasted 100× longer and produced a durable shift toward digital-democratic reform. The book attributes the difference to Taiwan's distinct cultural and political setting.

## Updates

### "From protest to demo"

From [[civic-ai-manifesto]] (Audrey Tang, Sep 2025): Tang condenses the Sunflower story into the slogan **from protest to demo** — "we didn't just protest. We pivoted to demonstration ('demo'). We occupied the parliament for three weeks and began building the system we wanted to see from the inside."

She specifies the technical scaffolding used: crowdsourced internet access for radical transparency, livestreamed debates, and *collaborative tools pioneered by other movements — such as Loomio (from Occupy Wellington) and later Polis (from Occupy Seattle)*. The trust-in-government number Tang reports: 9% in 2014 → 70%+ by 2020. "The best way to fix a system is to build a better one."

### Antisocial media as the precursor

From [[civic-ai-manifesto]]: the 2014 collapse of trust was "largely due to 'engagement through enragement' parasitic AI — what I call *antisocial media*." This framing reads the Sunflower as a defensive response to the same kind of AI dynamics that the [[Civic-AI]] 6-Pack is now designed to counter.
