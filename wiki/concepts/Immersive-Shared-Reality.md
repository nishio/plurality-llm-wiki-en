## Definition

From [[plurality-book]] chapter **5-2 Immersive Shared Reality** (ISR), this covers virtual reality (VR), augmented reality (AR), and other technologies that put multiple people into a shared multisensory environment. The book treats ISR as a high-depth point on the depth-vs-breadth spectrum: closer to intimacy than to voting, but with the potential to bridge greater distances than physical co-presence allows.

## Why include ISR in a Plural agenda

ISR can support:

- shared physical context across great distance,
- non-verbal cues (gesture, gaze, body language) that are lost in text,
- the building of trust and intimacy needed to make richer collaboration possible (see [[Augmented-Deliberation]] for what conversation alone cannot deliver).

The Park of Aging at the National Museum of Emerging Science and Innovation (Miraikan) in Tokyo opens the chapter as an illustration: visitors don sensory dampers and body weights to inhabit an older person's perceptual world — an empathy-building immersive experience.

## Risks the book flags

[[plurality-book]] is explicit that ISR comes with severe risks:

- **Virtual escapism** — Snow Crash / Ready Player One dystopias of retreat from civic life;
- **Diminished physical health** — extended ISR use, sedentary lifestyle, isolation;
- **Digital divide** — uneven access to ISR widens existing inequality;
- **Physical health implications** — vision and posture;
- **Corporate control, surveillance, monopolization** — without Plural governance and open protocols, ISR becomes "the most iron monopolistic cages we have known";
- **Identity and authenticity** — the freedom to inhabit any persona complicates trust.

The chapter concludes that a "headlong rush" into a monopolistic metaverse must be prevented by reading ISR alongside [[Identity-and-Personhood]], [[Association-and-Plural-Publics]] and the rest of the [[Plurality]] stack.
