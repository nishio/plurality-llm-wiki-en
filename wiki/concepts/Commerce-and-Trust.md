## Definition

From [[plurality-book]] chapter **4-3 Commerce and Trust**, Plural commerce rests on **quantitative trust networks** rather than a single universal fungible currency. Trust in a Plural setting is:

- **quantitative** — calibrated to who is making which claim about whom,
- **networked** — propagated along social-graph paths (six degrees of separation, "max-flow" trust between two nodes),
- **community-rooted** — denominated in community currencies, reputation tokens, or interpersonal "favors owed".

## Why this matters

The book contrasts this with conventional money, which collapses heterogeneous value into a single scalar. A scalar currency is high-bandwidth in breadth (it works between strangers) but throws away the rich information embedded in **who trusts whom about what**. Plural commerce aims to keep that information legible without giving up on long-distance trade.

## Mechanisms the book discusses

- **Community currencies** — local or community-issued moneys (the book cites a long tradition).
- **Social currencies** — likes, friends, citations, network centrality used as proxies for value within a community.
- **Reputation and credit networks** — replacing fungible payment with the redemption of "favors owed" along a network of debt and trust, computed via algorithms like max-flow.
- **Trust Over IP Foundation** and start-ups like **Holochain** as concrete protocol efforts.
- **Verifying Identity as a Social Intersection** (Immorlica, Jackson, Weyl 2019) — using social-graph position to verify identity claims.

## Underpinning later chapters

The chapter explicitly sets up [[Property-and-Contract]] (chapter 4-4) and [[Social-Markets]] (chapter 5-7) as the next logical steps: once commerce is built on networked trust rather than anonymous money, joint use of digital assets and Plural market formulas like [[Quadratic-Funding]] become tractable.

## Africa as a leading case

The book singles out African communities — which "indigenously invented mobile money" — as natural early adopters, given existing trust-based, open-source social systems and rapid mobile-fintech adoption.
