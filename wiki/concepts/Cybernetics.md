## In the book

[[plurality-book]] mentions cybernetics briefly in chapter **3-2 [[Connected-Society]]**:

> "[[Norbert-Wiener]] coined the term 'cybernetics' for a field studying such rich interactive systems. By perceiving the limits of the box of modernity they highlighted even as they helped construct it, these pioneers helped us imagine a social world outside it..."

Wiener and cybernetics are placed alongside [[Georg-Simmel]]'s "web" and [[John-Dewey]]'s pragmatist critique of state institutions as part of the **20th-century pre-history of Plurality** — earlier attempts to articulate a non-monist, network-aware vision of social and technical systems.

The book does not develop cybernetics in detail; it appears only as a thread in the genealogy chapter.

## Background

Cybernetics is the trans-disciplinary study of regulatory and feedback systems, defined by [[Norbert-Wiener]] in *Cybernetics: Or Control and Communication in the Animal and the Machine* (1948) as the study of control and communication in animals, machines, and society. It informed early computer science, ecology, anthropology (Bateson, Mead), management theory (Stafford Beer's Cybersyn in 1970s Chile), and was a direct ancestor of contemporary systems science and complexity theory.
