## In the book

[[plurality-book]] uses [[Shoshana-Zuboff]]'s "Surveillance Capitalism" as the **opening epigraph** of chapter **2-0** "Information Technology and Democracy: a Widening Gulf":

> "Surveillance capitalism is...a coup from above...an overthrow of the people's sovereignty and a prominent force in the perilous drift towards democratic deconsolidation..."

Zuboff's diagnosis is invoked again in the section discussing the **centralizing threat** to democracy — privacy collapse, concentration of statistical / model-training capacity in a few firms, and the rise of "AI race" dynamics that pressure all societies to accept similar concentration.

Surveillance Capitalism is not treated as a Plural concept of its own. The book uses Zuboff's framing as the most influential critical diagnosis of the present, and then argues that the Plural response must be **systemic** (Plural identity, association, [[Data-Coalitions]], DPI) rather than merely individual privacy enforcement (the EU / GDPR direction).

## Background

"Surveillance Capitalism" is the term [[Shoshana-Zuboff]] popularized in *The Age of Surveillance Capitalism: The Fight for a Human Future at the New Frontier of Power* (PublicAffairs, 2019). The argument: contemporary tech firms harvest human experience as a "behavioral surplus", convert it into prediction products, and trade those products in "behavioral futures markets", in a process that escapes both market discipline and democratic accountability.
