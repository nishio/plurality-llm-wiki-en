## In the book

[[plurality-book]] coins "monist atomism" in chapter **3-0 What is Plurality?** as the shared root the book identifies under both of its foil ideologies:

> "This contrasts with a common element between [[Libertarianism]] and [[Technocracy]]: that both consider the world to be made up of atoms (viz. individuals) and a social whole, a view we call 'monist atomism'."

The book uses monist atomism as a diagnostic device throughout:

- **[[Libertarianism]]** takes the atomic individual as primary and treats society as the (derivative) aggregate;
- **[[Technocracy]]** takes the unified social whole (a single welfare function, a planner, an AGI) as primary and treats individuals as interchangeable nodes;
- both miss "the core idea of 數位 Plurality, that intersecting diverse social groups and the diverse and collaborative people whose identities are constituted by these intersections are the core fabric of the social world".

Chapter **3-1 Living in a Plural World** extends the critique into natural science: monist atomism is also the worldview Gödel, quantum mechanics, ecology, and network sociology have moved past. The book argues that contemporary [[Generative-Foundation-Models]] / Technocracy are running on a metaphysics already superseded by the sciences they invoke for authority.

## Why it matters

Monist atomism is not just an abstract label; it organizes the book's positive program. Plural [[Identity-and-Personhood]], [[Association-and-Plural-Publics]], [[Plural-Voting]], and [[Social-Markets]] are each presented as institutional alternatives to atomic-individualist (Libertarian) or unified-whole (Technocratic) defaults.
