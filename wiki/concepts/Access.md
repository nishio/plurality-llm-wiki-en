## Definition

From [[plurality-book]] chapter **4-5 Access**, [[Plurality]] cannot be realized if large parts of the population are excluded from the digital substrate on which it runs. Access in the Plural sense covers:

- physical connectivity (bandwidth, devices),
- inclusion in [[Digital-Public-Infrastructure]] (digital ID, payments, basic government services),
- the literacy and competence to use them,
- removal of "digital divides" inside countries and between them.

## Three exemplars

The chapter develops the access story through Taiwan, Estonia and Ukraine:

- **Taiwan** — Plural Innovation strategy, civil-society-driven digital services.
- **Estonia** — e-Estonia, the e-residency program, and the mRiik state app launched as a successor to Diia.
- **Ukraine** — the Diia app, developed under Mykhailo Fedorov (the book also flags Ukraine's wartime willingness to share code and UX with Estonia).

The book treats these as proofs of concept that "fundamental functions and democratic essence" of Digital Public Infrastructure can be similar across jurisdictions even as each adapts.

## Access as a right

The chapter argues access is a **right**, not a market amenity — alongside but interlocking with identity, association, commerce, and property. Like other rights, it is partly aspirational; positive rights (e.g., universal quality bandwidth) are outside the immediate capacity of many states but remain a measure of democratic ambition.

## Open Questions

- Where does Access sit when state-issued ID is a precondition for using public services, and the same state has authoritarian tendencies?
- The book treats Estonia, Taiwan and Ukraine as success cases; how transferable are they to states with weaker civil society or less digital literacy?
