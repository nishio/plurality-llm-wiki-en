## In the book

[[plurality-book]] mentions Universal Basic Income (UBI) only briefly in chapter **2-0**: in the Technocratic vision, since AI-driven abundance "may not be equally distributed", "a key element of the technocratic social vision is therefore material redistribution, usually through a 'universal basic income' (UBI)."

The book treats UBI as the canonical [[Technocracy]] policy lever — paired with strong central control over AI access — and contrasts it implicitly with Plurality's preference for **collective / group-level** structures like [[Data-Coalitions]], [[Quadratic-Funding]], and [[Social-Markets]] that distribute value through productive participation rather than as a per-capita transfer. [[Andrew-Yang]]'s campaigns are noted as the vehicle by which UBI entered US mainstream politics.

The book does not engage with UBI's full policy literature; it is mentioned only to characterize the Technocratic position.

## Background

A universal basic income is a periodic, unconditional cash payment delivered to all members of a political community, regardless of work status or other means tests. Modern advocacy spans figures as different as Milton Friedman (as a "negative income tax"), Philippe Van Parijs, Charles Murray, and [[Sam-Altman]]; pilots have been run in Finland, Kenya (GiveDirectly), Stockton CA, and elsewhere.
