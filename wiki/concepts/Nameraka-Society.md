## Origin in the manuscript

Nameraka Society (なめらかな社会, *Nameraka-na Shakai*, "smooth society") was added to the English manuscript of [[plurality-book]] by [[pluralitybook-upstream-2024-2026]] **PR #1055** (Taiyou, merged 2025-01-23) as a ~37-line new sub-section of chapter 3-3 [[The-Lost-Dao]] titled **"Quality Control and Nameraka Society"**.

Before PR #1055 the concept was absent from the English manuscript. It is canonically a *Japanese* concept articulated by [[Ken-Suzuki]] in the 2000s and consolidated in his 2013 book *The Nameraka Society and its Enemies* (なめらかな社会とその敵, Keiso Shobo).

## The sub-section's argument

The PR #1055 sub-section makes a deliberate **parallel construction** to the main [[The-Lost-Dao]] argument:

1. Wiener's cybernetics is *not only* the ARPA / IPTO / internet lineage — it also flowed eastward via Deming's Quality Control lectures to Japan (1950, sponsored by the **Union of Japanese Scientists and Engineers / JUSE**).
2. JUSE established the **Deming Prize** in 1951 to recognise QC contributions. Toyota adopted process-centric QC in the early 1950s, won the Deming Prize in 1965, and evolved its practices into what we now call **PDCA cycle (Plan-Do-Check-Act)** and **Kaizen**. The book frames PDCA / Kaizen as "one of the achievements demonstrating Wiener's cybernetic concept of feedback loops for self-adaptation."
3. Wiener's feedback loops also seeded the 1980s **complex systems** field (Stuart Kauffman et al.). Suzuki sits at the intersection of complex systems research, artificial life, and entrepreneurship.
4. Suzuki extends cybernetics + complex systems beyond manufacturing **into society itself**: Nameraka Society.

## The vision

Per the manuscript text, Suzuki sees the world as **a complex web-like network** in which two scale-free patterns repeatedly emerge:

- **Membrane**: like a cell wall, creates an inside/outside boundary. At social scale this becomes the modern nation-state's strict citizenship rule.
- **Nucleus**: controls the cell via few-degree-of-freedom parameters (DNA). At the social scale this becomes state power and authority; at the individual scale it becomes the ego that distinguishes self from other.

Because membrane and nucleus repeat at every scale, "our society is also formed as membranes (separation of inside and outside) and nuclei (power and authority), preventing the resolution of binary oppositions."

Nameraka Society is the project to **dissolve membrane and nucleus through technology** so that:

- individuals are no longer rigid units but **dividuals** (分人) — multi-agent systems constructed through the collaboration of multiple cells (drawing on Deleuze's "dividual" as opposed to the indivisible "individual");
- people belong to multiple communities **simultaneously** without society expecting a single coherent identity;
- Carl Schmitt's friend / enemy distinction is overcome by "smooth technological management of violence."

The manuscript notes that Suzuki's project predates Ethereum (2014): around 2005 he proposed **Constructive Social Contract Theory** — a society under law "automatically executed using human-and-machine-readable legal languages."

## Plurality reading the book offers

Suzuki "seems to believe that for [[John-Dewey|Dewey]]'s emergent publics to flourish, the source of power itself must be emergent." Two concrete proposals follow from Nameraka thinking:

- **Dividual democracy** (a.k.a. divicracy, 分人民主主義) — see [[Liquid-Democracy]] §"Related concept: divicracy" and [[Plural-Voting]].
- **PICSY** (Propagational Investment Currency SYstem) — see [[Social-Markets]] §"Frontiers of social markets".

And [[Takahiro-Anno]]'s 2024 Tokyo campaign is named as a contemporary realisation in the same lineage.

## "Another lost Dao"

The sub-section closes:

> "Suzuki's vision for applying these insights to today's digital, networked world can be seen as a project to rediscover and reconstruct **'another lost dao'**. While these developments occurred largely in parallel to and separate from the internet evolution, they represent another path toward realizing cybernetic thinking."

This is the manuscript's explicit naming of Nameraka as **a second [[The-Lost-Dao|Lost Dao]] parallel to the principal Licklider / Engelbart / Nelson lineage**. Notice that the principal Lost Dao is set in the US and is about networked computers; the "another lost Dao" is set in Japan and is about industrial organization extended into social theory. Both are framed as cybernetic descendants.

## Open Questions

- The book footnotes a **Simmel ↔ Deleuze bridging note** (PR #1059): "ジンメルの交差的(非)個人との共通性もみられる。ジンメルが社会的関係性からdividualを見たのに加えたが、鈴木はより生物学的視点から考察を試みたドゥルーズの"dividual"から着想している。" This is rendered in Japanese inside an English-language manuscript — a notable wiki森 observation. The intent is that Simmel approached dividual *from social relationships*, whereas Suzuki took inspiration from Deleuze's *more biological viewpoint*. The EN manuscript thus contains a Japanese-language footnote about a Japan-side reading of the German tradition.
- The "another lost Dao" framing is rhetorically strong but theoretically informal: in what concrete sense does Japanese PDCA / Kaizen / Nameraka constitute a "loss"? PDCA and Kaizen were industrially **adopted, not lost**, in Japan and Toyota. The "loss" appears to be a *missed social-theoretical extension* — Suzuki's Nameraka — rather than a literal loss like ARPA-era networking. The book leaves this asymmetry unaddressed.
- Whether the Nameraka vision is more naturally a precursor to [[Plurality]], an alternative to it, or a complement: the book's choice to file it under [[The-Lost-Dao]] suggests *precursor*, but the [[Liquid-Democracy]] and [[Social-Markets]] footnotes treat divicracy and PICSY as *technical mechanisms* available to Plurality. Both readings coexist in the manuscript.
