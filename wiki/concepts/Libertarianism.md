## In the book

[[plurality-book]] uses "Libertarianism" — abbreviated from *Civilization VI*'s "Corporate Libertarianism" — as the second of two foil ideologies (alongside [[Technocracy]]) that [[Plurality]] positions itself against. Chapter **2-0** characterizes the Libertarian view as:

- the belief that **cryptography and networking protocols can supplant** "human collective organization and politics", letting individuals participate in markets free from government and other collective "coercion";
- a strong investment in fictional inspirations — [[Ayn-Rand]], [[Neal-Stephenson]]'s *Snow Crash* / *Cryptonomicon*, and explicit canon texts: *The Sovereign Individual* by James Dale Davidson and Lord William Rees-Mogg, [[Curtis-Yarvin]]'s *Unqualified Reservations* (as "Mencius Moldbug"), [[Balaji-Srinivasan]]'s *The Network State* (see [[Network-State]]), *Bronze Age Mindset*;
- a peak moment in the 1990s — [[John-Perry-Barlow]]'s "Declaration of the Independence of Cyberspace", *The Sovereign Individual*, Stephenson's novels;
- a central intellectual / financial hub in [[Peter-Thiel]] and a complicated alliance with the nationalist / far-right via figures like Jacob Rees-Mogg, Sebastian Kurz, Blake Masters, and J. D. Vance.

The book also calls out an **"accelerationist"** sub-current, advocated by Yarvin, Davidson and Rees-Mogg, that sees the "nationalist backlash" as a useful accelerant to dissolve the nation-state.

## Why it is a foil for Plurality

Like [[Technocracy]], Libertarianism is — per [[plurality-book]] — grounded in **[[Monist-Atomism]]**: society is fundamentally a heap of atomic individuals, and collective structure is at best a contractual epiphenomenon. The book's specific Plural counter-claim: identity is not atomic, social value is supermodular rather than additive, and "exit / cryptography" cannot stand in for the deliberation and bridging that diverse societies require.
