## Definition

Digital Public Infrastructure (DPI) is the term [[plurality-book]] (especially chapter **7-0 Policy**) uses for digital identity, payments, data exchange, and basic service rails that are:

- developed and maintained as a public good (by states, civil society, or transnational networks),
- governed openly,
- interoperable across providers and jurisdictions.

The book treats DPI as the natural twenty-first-century counterpart of physical infrastructure (roads, electric grids, water systems) and as a precondition for both [[Digital-Democracy]] and [[Social-Markets]].

## Leading examples

- **India Stack** — Aadhaar identity + UPI payments + DigiLocker, taken as the most-developed DPI globally.
- **Taiwan** — Plural Innovation strategy, civic-tech infrastructure, Ministry of Digital Affairs.
- **Estonia** — e-Estonia, e-residency, the mRiik app.
- **Ukraine** — Diia.
- **European Digital Identity** and **Gaia-X** (data-sharing infrastructure).
- **Brazil and Singapore** — successful similar experiments cited by the book.

## Policy proposal

In Section 7, [[plurality-book]] argues governments and charities should devote roughly **1% of GDP** to digital public R&D, protocols and infrastructure — about a trillion US dollars a year globally, or roughly half current global IT investment — at least as much as currently flows into AI and crypto. This would be an increase of about two orders of magnitude over today's public open-source funding.

Funding should flow trans-nationally (through networks of digital ministries, civil society, and standards bodies) rather than purely nationally, matching the global nature of the protocols.

## Why this matters for Plurality

DPI is treated as the substrate that lets [[Identity-and-Personhood]], [[Association-and-Plural-Publics]], [[Commerce-and-Trust]], [[Property-and-Contract]] and [[Access]] all become available to ordinary people without depending on a handful of private platforms. Without DPI, these layers either don't exist or are quasi-monopolized by corporations or states. With DPI, [[The-Lost-Dao]] can be recovered.

## Open Questions

- DPI in authoritarian hands can be deeply harmful (universal surveillance). What design and governance conditions distinguish Plural DPI from surveillance infrastructure?
- The 1%-of-GDP target is aspirational; what political coalitions can mobilize that scale of public investment in software?
